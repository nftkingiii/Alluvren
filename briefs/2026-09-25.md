# Alluvren — product upgrades (2026-09-25)

## 1. Investors now get private outcomes (v0.2.0)
- When a redemption batch is finalized, each investor automatically receives two private records:
  - an **entitlement** for the units they were allocated;
  - an **outstanding record** for the units the window could not fund.
- Only that investor can see their records. Other investors, the operator and the reviewers cannot.
- Investors **acknowledge** an entitlement or **withdraw** outstanding units themselves, once each; repeats are rejected. Both produce a receipt, clearly labelled as a demo acknowledgment (no money moves).
- If finalization is rejected, no investor records are created.
- Verified on BitSafe LocalNet through a real governed finalization, not just in tests.

## 2. Approval rules are now a governed fund policy (v0.3.0)
Built around the five parties the interviews described: COO, Operations, Treasury, Compliance and the Administrator.
- **Flexible roles and quorums:** a fund policy on the ledger lists each role, who may fill it, and how many must approve (e.g. Treasury 1-of-2, COO final sign-off). New roles: Compliance, Administrator check, Final sign-off.
- **Stricter separation of duties:** an approver cannot be an investor in the batch, cannot approve under two roles, and cannot be the proposer or operator. Extra or duplicate approvals are rejected, so the approved set is exact.
- **Sign-off that scales with risk:** the ledger works out extra requirements from the batch itself, so the backend cannot skip them:
  - funded below 50% → Compliance must approve;
  - one investor receiving over 80% of the liquidity → Treasury needs 2 approvals;
  - any exception flagged on the batch → Compliance must approve.
- **Rule changes are governed:** creating or changing the policy requires the BitSafe member threshold, versions advance one at a time, and a change blocks batches approved under the old rules until they are re-frozen.
- **Existing batches keep working:** batches created before the policy use the original two-reviewer rule, now with a conflict-of-interest check added.
- Status: built, tested locally (all rule checks pass, and each safeguard was shown to be caught if removed), and verified on BitSafe LocalNet: creating and changing the policy needed the member threshold, a 40%-funded batch was blocked until Compliance approved, and a batch approved under the old policy was blocked after the policy changed.

## 3. Signed-in users with enforced roles (Gate 8)
- People now sign in to a **Live ledger** workspace. Each account maps to one ledger party and its roles (reviewer roles, operator, BitSafe member, investor).
- **Reviewers** approve or revoke their own approvals on the real ledger; **the operator** proposes finalization with the chosen approvals; **BitSafe members** confirm and execute from their own node, and execution only runs once the threshold is met.
- **Investors** see and act on only their own records; they cannot see the full batch list.
- The server, not the browser, decides who you act as: no request can switch party, node or approval set. Ledger rejections come back in plain words (e.g. "Missing required approvals for role ComplianceReviewer").
- The live tab is clearly labelled as LocalNet; the simulated desk keeps its demo label.
- Status: built and tested locally (all security checks pass, and each safeguard was shown to be caught if removed). Sign-in is a temporary password login; wallet sign-in will follow once Grofty replies. The run against LocalNet is next.

## 4. Clearer live workspace and a refreshed design
- **Each batch shows what it still needs:** a checklist of required approvals with ticks, and the reason when a rule adds one ("Required because funded below 50%").
- **A funding bar** shows how much of each redemption was funded, with a marker at the policy's 50% line, so a shortfall is visible at a glance.
- **Each role sees its next step** at the top ("1 batch needs your Treasury approval", "1 proposal is ready to execute").
- Reviewers see "You approved · Revoke" instead of a second Approve button; the operator proposes with every valid approval and is told what's missing; BitSafe proposals use plain names with a confirmation progress bar.
- The signed-in account and a LocalNet label sit in the header; the simulated screens are grouped under "Walkthrough · simulated".
- Design: editorial serif titles, larger minimum text, one consistent colour system (gold only for the main action), content that stays at the top of the page and doesn't stretch across wide screens.
